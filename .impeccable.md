# False Nine

## Design Context

### Users
Groups of friends, mostly 18–35, playing on their own phones around a table in a
pub, flat or car. One person hosts, the rest join with a code. Screens are read at
arm's length, often in low light, between conversations. Attention is shared with
the people in the room, so every screen has to be legible in a glance and every
primary action reachable with a thumb.

### Brand Personality
Composed, premium, quietly confident. The work of a studio that gets out of the
way: the artwork is the drama, the interface is the frame around it. Warm rather
than clinical, because it is a game played with mates, but never loud, never
busy, never shouting for attention while people are talking to each other.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Reference: the three card designs in `public/assets` (`Imposter_GPT.png`,
  `tictactoe_GPT.png`, `Draft_GPT.png`). The UI has to look like it came out of
  the same studio.
- Apple's dark-mode language, honestly applied: a cool near-black ground, panels
  made of a thin white wash over it, one hairline per edge, soft shadow, generous
  continuous corners, big pill buttons.
- Colour is disciplined. Gold is the action colour everywhere. Each game owns a
  hue — crimson, teal, royal — used only as atmosphere: the lights behind a
  screen, the tint on its card, the Play Now pill on its own card. Red means
  something is wrong; green means ready or confirmed. Nothing else is coloured.
- Type: San Francisco on Apple devices, self-hosted Inter everywhere else, on
  Apple's scale. Identity comes from the two-tone title (white then gold) and
  the wide letterspaced eyebrow, not from a novelty face.
- Anti-references: glassmorphism and heavy blur, neumorphism, bevels and metal,
  neon, casino and betting sites, purple gradients, gradient text, a dashboard,
  identical radii on everything, cards inside cards.

### Design Principles
1. Surface, accent, glow. Every element is one of three things: a panel, a hue
   scoped to a subtree, or a soft light behind. No fourth material.
2. Gold is the verb. It marks the thing to do next and nothing else.
3. The artwork carries the drama. The interface stays quiet so it can.
4. Depth from light, not from edges. One hairline, one shadow, light from above.
   Never a bevel, never two borders.
5. Drama scales with stakes. Setup and voting stay flat and readable; the peek,
   the reveal and the result are allowed a moment.
6. Phone first, thumb first. Primary actions in the bottom third, 44pt targets,
   nothing critical below the fold.
7. Motion is a state change, not decoration. Nothing loops while people talk.
8. State is never colour alone. Every chip says its state in a word.
